desc:
- Vertical pulling is one of the gentlest ways to introduce pulling with a full range
  of motion.
- 'The high angle makes this movement a lot easier than Horizontal or Full Pullups. '
- "Although these may feel very easy, it\u2019s useful to practice these for a few\
  \ weeks to feel how your joints want move and build healthy movement patterns."
- "They\u2019re also a great therapy exercise. The gentle movement stretches stiff\
  \ muscles and heals old wounds."
- Keep your shoulder blades down and back to better engage your back muscles. Learning
  to do this with straight arms at the bottom of the movement will build fundamental
  scapular strength - very important for advanced calisthenics!
form:
- "Slow and steady wins the race on this exercise. Don\u2019t just speed through these.\
  \ Find a controlled, rhythmic pace to really feel this movement. 2 Seconds Down,\
  \ 1 Second Pause, 2 Seconds Up. Repeat."
- Keep your hands around sternum height. Your fist should touch your chest at the
  top of the movement.
- Regardless of where you do this exercise, make sure you have a good grip on the
  object. A slippery surface will make holding on difficult, which is not the goal
  of this exercise.
- "Keep your body firm. Don\u2019t arch your body at the bottom to make the motion\
  \ easier. Core strength is important for all calisthenics, and this is a great place\
  \ to start building it."

tutorial:
- "Stand at arm\u2019s length facing a pole, tree, or wall section."
- Firmly grasp the object with both arms. Your arms should be straight.
- Ascend slowly until the object or your hands gently touch your chest.
- Pause for 1 Second.
- Slowly come back down.
- Pause for 1 Second.
- Repeat 3-6.
